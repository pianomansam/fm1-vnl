# VNL-1 Workbench

A local page for auditioning the song engine: the engine (app/song, app/engine) built to WebAssembly and played in an
AudioWorklet. Local only; not the FM-1, so it may exceed the device's budgets.

    sh workbench/build.sh            # -> app/build/workbench/ (wb.wasm and the page)
    python3 workbench/server.py      # http://localhost:8732/ (127.0.0.1 only; a port as the argument)
    node workbench/tools/smoke.mjs   # the engine glue's tests, no browser

## How it fits

- `engine/wb.c` (`wb.h`): the glue the page calls. One song plays; the page stages changes (roll fields, the custom
  set's instruments, the per-song overrides, the kit, a kick phrase; a seed too) and `wb_apply` commits them: only a
  commit is heard, at the next bar line by catch-up: the song
  restarts silently with the staged state, ticks to that bar, and sound resumes, so every state is as if the song had
  always had those decisions. A seed or a new arrangement map restarts from bar 1; a change in the last bar does too.
  At its end, as on the device (the design spec): 2-4 s of true silence (`app.c`'s, from the seed), then the next song,
  which the page rolls in the silence as "Re-roll all unlocked" does (the locks kept; `Desk.nextSong`) and which lands
  as the silence ends (`wb_advance`: the silence holds until it comes). With "Loop section" checked the page rolls
  nothing and the song never moves on.
- `wb_consts()` exports the engine's indices (RF_*, WB_OV_*, the roles, LIB_SET_WB) as JSON, so the page never keeps
  its own copy.
- `wb-worklet.js` runs wb.wasm (base/web/emu/worklet.js's pattern, without the device); `wb.js` is the page's side;
  `index.html` the page: the transport, then the decision rows by part (below).
- The page by part (Sam, 2026-11-05; what the workbench can do should later drive the device's screen and four knobs):
  `rows.js`'s `SECTIONS`, top to bottom like a track: Song (seed, key, tempo, mood, motion; under "more" the mode,
  swing, map, progression, chord color and the family), Drums, Bass, Comp, Pad, Lead, Texture. Each part's header
  carries the part's controls (mute and solo, listening only; ◀ 🎲 ▶ through its rolls and which roll it is; its
  lock; 👍 / 👎 on the roll heard; the drums' "Edit kit & pattern") and a one-line summary of its values (`summary()`:
  "Rhodes · Tresillo · mid register"). A part shows its instrument and its rhythm or style (the drums: kit and
  pattern); the rest folds under its "more", open or folded per viewer (localStorage `wb-more`, when the browser
  allows it; folded without). A part's roll row (phrase, voicings, bass line, pad notes, drum variation) shows the
  roll; its 🎲 and lock are the header's. The keys part is the Comp part in the page's text; the engine's names
  (`V_CHORD`, `keys_*`), the row ids and the snapshot's part key (`keys`) stay.
- `rows.js`: the rows, what each dropdown offers (from `options.json`; retired options only behind "show retired"),
  the dice (an approved option; a kit of the song's family: the family chooses the drums only) and what a value forces (a row it moves, or the lock that blocks
  it). `desk.js` drives the engine with them: every change is staged, checked against the locks (undone whole if a
  locked row would move) and landed; a seed or map change stages every locked or hand-set row again. The lead register's read-back runs scratch songs in a second wb.wasm on the main
  thread (`wb.js`'s probe), never on the audio thread.
- The drums: a kit row value from 16 is a preset over the drum lanes (`app/drums/wb_drums.c`): the synth kits (808 and
  CR-78, FOMAJAM's modeled machines; Crunchy, Boom-bap, Jazz and Phonk machine, from Optimist's LO-FI, BOOMBAP, JAZZ
  and PHONK kits: the Phonk machine a candidate, never rolled) and the shipped sampled kits (Room - big and Room - brushes, from
  Big Rusty's sticks and brushes; Dusty, from Gogodze, retired 2026-11-10 and out of the bank), reduced into the sample bank the
  device plays (`tools/samples/pack.py`, from the files `tools/prep_kits.py` makes into `samples/`: see
  `samples/README.md`), compiled into wb.wasm with the rest of the bank, so the page and the FM-1 play the same.
- Costs (the workbench's own; `docs/cost.md` is the firmware's): a song's drums, any kit, take under 1% of real time
  (Node, 128-frame quanta: Soft brushes 0.26%, Room - big with the ride throughout 0.25%, every lane the 808 with
  an audition every quantum 0.87%; worst quantum about 0.5 ms, JIT and GC included, against the 2.9 ms quantum).
- `options.json`: the option lists (instruments, kits, comping, lead styles, families: hand-maintained; the drum
  patterns, kick phrases, strum patterns and comp rhythms: generated by `tools/patterns/import.py` from
  `patterns/`), each approved, candidate or retired, seeded from the ledgers. A snapshot naming a retired lead style
  (the phrase planner or the hybrid, removed with the lead styles) restores on a rolled style and says so; rows it
  names that are gone (the lead-doubling switch, the lead arpeggio rows) are ignored.
- Candidates (`patterns/README.md`): a new pattern imported from `patterns/` is offered, marked "(candidate)", in its
  row (Pattern, Progression, Kick phrase; a comp rhythm or strum pattern in Rhythm) and plays when picked; the
  roll and the dice never draw it. Under it, **Approve** posts it to `/approve`, which writes
  `patterns/status.json`; `tools/patterns/import.sh` then makes the firmware draw it and rebuilds the workbench. A mode
  only a candidate progression suits can be picked too (its candidate comes with it). The strum pattern is greyed when
  the comp doesn't strum.
- Scale (the Mode row's id, "mode"; in the Song section's more rows): the six modes, Ionian to Aeolian. Songs were
  major or minor from 2026-11-05 until Sam undid it on 2026-11-06; a snapshot or feedback entry saved then restores on
  the mode its progression was listed in, with the same chords (`desk.js`'s `fromMajorMinor`: 45-48, split off then,
  go back to 5, 31, 33 and 41).
- Comping (the Rhythm row on keys): one place to pick the Comp part's comping (Sam, 2026-11-05), from one source:
  every comping rhythm in `patterns/comp-rhythms` (`options.json`'s generated `compRhythms`: Held, Half notes, Stabs, Held on 2, Held on 2
  and 4, Charleston, Tresillo, Late one; a new one marked "(candidate)" until approved), each only on the comp
  instruments its data names, then Strum (the guitars) and the retired styles (behind "show retired"). A fixed
  rhythm's value is its `CS_*` id (staged as the engine's comp override), an added one's 100 + its table index (the
  `comp_pat` override, the comp override cleared), so snapshots keep their values. A new comp instrument that doesn't
  play the rhythm picked puts the row back to as rolled, and one left on such an instrument (a snapshot, the
  engine's) greys the row (still open to pick another). Approve on a candidate rhythm writes `patterns/status.json`,
  as for strum. A snapshot from before (its Comp rhythm row) restores that rhythm in Comping. Light eighths is removed
  (Sam, 2026-11-05): never offered, not even behind "show retired"; a snapshot or feedback item that picked it
  restores as Held and says so. The roll's draw (each rhythm's weight, its busyness against the song's motion) is the
  engine's: `patterns/README.md`.
- The Comp part's one Rhythm row (Sam, 2026-11-05; `rows.js`'s `rhythmRow`): on keys it is the comping row above (the
  comp rhythms); on the guitars, which only strum, it is the strum pattern row (candidates marked, retired hidden, as
  in any row). The two stay rows of their own underneath (their overrides, locks and snapshot values), so snapshots
  and feedback restore as before; the page shows the one the comp instrument plays. A comp rhythm a guitar can't play
  (a snapshot's, the engine's) shows the comping row instead, greyed and open to change.
- Feedback: 👍/👎 and a note, on the song or a row, save a line to `workbench/feedback.jsonl`: a versioned snapshot
  (every row, the locks, the seed; `desk.snapshot()` / `desk.restore()`), where the song was, and the log of every applied
  change. Entries queue in localStorage while the server is down (the page says "not saved yet") and are resent.
  `node workbench/tools/replay.mjs <line number | snapshot.json> out.wav [seconds]` renders one through wb.wasm.
  The snapshot is the song being heard (a change still waiting for its bar line is under `pending`); `changes` are those
  since the previous entry, stamped with the position at the click; entries carry an id the server won't write twice;
  ones the server refuses (4xx) are kept in the browser's `wb-feedback-dead`. The server takes POSTs only from its own
  page (Host, Origin and a JSON content type are checked).
- `server.py` also takes `POST /feedback`, `/kit` and `/pattern` (names `[a-z0-9-]{1,40}`, bodies up to 1 MB), writing
  only under `workbench/`, and `POST /approve` (`{"family", "id"}`, a candidate of `patterns/model.json`; anything else is refused), writing
  `patterns/status.json` only; its tests: `python3 workbench/tools/test_server.py`.
- `server.py` serves the build first, then `workbench/` (kits, patterns, samples, options.json); `GET /list/kits` and
  `/list/patterns` give the saved names.
- The drum screen (`drums.js`; "Edit kit & pattern"): the kit, pattern and kick-phrase rows; per lane a sound from any
  approved kit (retired ones behind "show retired"), its 🎲 (a sound that kit ships on this lane, of the song's family),
  ▶ (the sound alone) and a level; the two-bar grid (`wb_pat_step`: a click cycles empty → hit → ghost; the song
  switches to the custom pattern and hears it from the next bar; a poly3 shaker, once edited, becomes a two-bar loop).
  A drawn grid is a hand edit of the pattern row (it survives a new seed; a locked pattern refuses it) and is in the
  snapshot (`drums.grid`). "Save as new kit / pattern" writes `workbench/kits|patterns/<name>.json` (the lanes or the
  grid, with their sounds named and the grid drawn: `x` hit, `o` ghost); "Load" plays one back.
  The grid shows the pattern as written: a song rolled below motion 35 plays the closed hat without its ghosts, and
  below 25 no closed or open hats at all, forced or edited pattern alike (calm songs thin the hats, 2026-11-05).

- The device mock ("Device screen (mock)", folded per viewer as `wb-more`'s "Device"): the FM-1's 240 x 240 screen at
  2x, its controls by printed label (plat.h's buttons, the SELECT, ALGO and PRESET encoders, K1-K4; clickable, a
  scroll on a dial, and keys: 1-4 the knobs, 5-7 the encoders, Shift for counter-clockwise, a letter per button, `[`
  `]` the OCT buttons), the screen reader's line under it, and the flow between the screens with the breadcrumb. It
  plays the song heard: a knob asks for a change that waits for the next 4-bar boundary as app.c's do (shown pending),
  then goes to the engine through the rows' own `Desk.set`; PLAY is the page's Play. All of it is in `ui/`, which
  `build.sh` doesn't copy, so the server serves it straight from disk:
  - `ui/screens.js`: the screens as data (bands and what they draw, the knobs, each control's action and where it
    leads, what the reader says; its header has the format). Edit it, then "Reload screens" (the song plays on) or
    reload the page. Its mistakes are listed under the flow and the last good screens stay.
  - `ui/device.js`: gfx.c's drawing (cv_begin, cv_text with its blending, text_w, the amber palette) and app.c's words
    (KEY_NAME ...), no DOM; `ui/panel.js` the page's side. `ui/fonts.js` is generated:
    `.venv/bin/python3 -I workbench/tools/export_font.py` (base/tools/gen_font.py's fonts as base/ has them).
  - The device runs the same screens (docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-06-device-screens.md): `ui/screens.js` is the one
    source. `ui/vocab.json` lists the draw items, actions and values both sides implement (`problems()` and the
    generator refuse anything else); `node workbench/tools/export_screens.mjs` writes `ui/screens.json`, and
    `.venv/bin/python -I tools/screens/gen.py` turns it, `rows.json` and `options.json` into `app/ui/*_gen.c/.h`, the
    tables app/ui's runtime reads (the value bag `values.c`, the drawing `render.c`, the navigator `nav.c`: the mock's
    `valuesOf`, `drawScreen` and `Device` in C). To iterate: edit `ui/screens.js` (and `device.js` for a new item or
    action, with its C twin in `app/ui/`), reload the mock, then run both tools and `./build.sh --host`; `node
    workbench/tools/parity.mjs [OUTDIR]` runs scripted presses on the simulator (`uiscreen`, `uistate`, `uirow`) and
    compares every screen with the mock pixel for pixel (OUTDIR: the PNGs, the device's beside the mock's).
    `tests/run_tests.sh` checks that both generated files are fresh and that the parity holds. The device runs every
    screen (app.h's `VNL_UI_RUNTIME`, 1); the Parts roll's notes come from the song itself, a copy run ahead of it
    (app.c), and the Sections screen's jumps, Loop and Stay on song from the song started again and fast-forwarded.
  - The song's facts the main screen shows (the section, the map's name, the chord as `th_chord_name` spells it) come
    from `wb_screen(bar)`.
  - The real FM-1 as the mock's controls (`ui/usb.js`, app.c's "browser's remote"; Chrome or Edge: Safari has no Web
    MIDI): "Connect FM-1" asks for MIDI with SysEx, finds the ports named "VNL-1 FM-1" and takes the FM-1's buttons,
    keys, encoders and knobs; the mock acts on them as on its own (a button or key as it goes down). Meanwhile the
    FM-1 stops its song, stays silent and shows BROWSER / CONTROLLING THE WORKBENCH; it acts on nothing itself.
    "Disconnect FM-1", closing the tab or unplugging gives it back (stopped, the song held: PLAY continues it). The protocol, SysEx prefix F0 7D 56 4E
    ("VN", apart from base's remote 7D 4C 43, log 7D 4C 47, WiFi 7D 4C 57, the screen reader 7D 53 52 and the
    update's framing that the updater and the mirror use), one message an event:
    - browser to FM-1: `F0 7D 56 4E 01 F7` remote on, re-sent each second as the keepalive (the FM-1 lets go after
      3 s without one, or at a new USB connection); `F0 7D 56 4E 02 F7` remote off.
    - FM-1 to browser: `F0 7D 56 4E 11 ss 01 F7` the state (ss 1 on: the answer to each remote on; 0 off; 01 the
      protocol); `F0 7D 56 4E 20 bb ss F7` button bb (plat.h's `B_*`: FX 0 ... OCT+ 13) down (1) or up (0);
      `F0 7D 56 4E 21 ee dd F7` encoder ee (0 SELECT, 1 ALGO, 2 PRESET, 3-6 K1-K4) turned dd detents (7-bit two's
      complement, -64..63; + clockwise; a fast turn's detents add up, sent as the MIDI out ring has room);
      `F0 7D 56 4E 22 kk ss F7` key kk (0..26, plat.h's: 0 = F3) down (1) or up (0): the mock acts on KEYkk as it
      goes down (its song map's sections), the up ignored. The FM-1 acts on none of them itself.

The smoke test is the evidence that the engine renders and lands changes; whether it sounds right needs a listen in
Chrome or Safari.
